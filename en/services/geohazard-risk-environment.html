01 · SCOPE

Ground that moves, water that sweeps away, soil that washes off.

Identifying geomorphological and environmental hazards, quantifying risk and designing mitigation measures. A cross-cutting line for every project that requires a permit, land-use planning or an emergency plan.

What we deliver

  • Hazard zoning (high, medium, low)
  • Vulnerability and risk assessment
  • GIS maps of land suitability
  • Structural and non-structural measures
  • EIA studies and environmental management plans (PMA)
  • Support for ANLA / CAR permits
  • Emergency and contingency plans

When it is critical

  • Municipal POMCA / POT / EOT (watershed and land-use plans)
  • Environmental permit for infrastructure
  • Lot acquisition in mountainous areas
  • Road projects in unstable areas
  • Post-disaster reconstruction
  • Land-use planning
02 · HAZARDS

Identify it before it happens.

  1. Mass movementsLandslides, flows, rockfall. Geomorphological characterization, identification of scarps, triggering factors (rainfall, earthquakes, human activity).
  2. Floods and debris flowsWatershed analysis, flood maps by return period, detection of areas on alluvial floodplains.
  3. Erosion and gulliesSheet erosion, rills, active gullies. Analysis of land use, vegetation cover and degradation potential.
  4. Earthquakes and liquefactionSeismic microzonation, identification of potentially liquefiable soils, local amplification.
  5. Sinkholes and subsidenceNatural causes (caverns, dissolution) or human causes (water extraction, mining). Monitoring and characterization.
03 · VULNERABILITY AND RISK

Risk = Hazard × Vulnerability.

Identifying the hazard is not enough. Vulnerability has to be quantified (how exposed and fragile everything in the area is) to understand the real risk and prioritize actions.

Vulnerability analysis

  • Inventory of exposed elements
  • Physical vulnerability (structures)
  • Social vulnerability (population)
  • Economic vulnerability
  • Environmental vulnerability

Risk estimation

  • Qualitative risk (traffic-light matrix)
  • Quantitative risk (expected losses)
  • Risk maps by scenario
  • Land suitability for each land use
  • Construction restrictions by zone
04 · MITIGATION AND RESTORATION

Reduce the risk, restore the land.

Structural measures

  • Slope stabilization
  • Drainage and subdrainage works
  • Walls, gabions, groynes
  • Dikes, levees, detention ponds
  • Geomorphological restoration

Non-structural measures

  • Land-use regulation
  • Resettlement of exposed population
  • Early warning systems
  • Education and emergency plans
  • Bioengineering (revegetation)
05 · ENVIRONMENTAL STUDIES

EIA, PMA and permitting support.

06 · PROCESS

From assessment to an actionable plan.

  1. Secondary information · 2 weeksCompilation of existing information: official cartography, geology, hydrology, IDEAM (Colombian hydrometeorological institute) weather data.
  2. Field work · 2-4 weeksGeomorphological reconnaissance, survey of critical zones, photo log, geolocation of points.
  3. GIS analysis · 2 weeksLayer building, multi-criteria analysis, hazard modeling, thematic map generation.
  4. Risk assessment · 2 weeksHazard × vulnerability overlay. Definition of levels. Identification of critical exposed elements.
  5. Management plan · 2-3 weeksDesign of structural and non-structural measures. Schedule, budget, monitoring indicators.
  6. Delivery and technical defenseSigned technical document. We defend it before the CAR, ANLA, the municipality or the relevant agency.
