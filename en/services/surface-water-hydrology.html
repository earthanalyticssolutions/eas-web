01 · SCOPE

Water rules. So does modeling it.

Before installing a culvert, sizing a bridge, developing a lot near a river or applying for an environmental permit, you need to know how much water arrives, when it arrives and how it moves. EAS models hydrologic and hydraulic behavior with industry-standard software and verifiable hydrometeorological data from IDEAM (Colombian Institute of Hydrology, Meteorology and Environmental Studies).

What we deliver

  • Design discharges by return period
  • Flood maps and hazard extents
  • Hydraulic design of structures (bridges, box culverts)
  • Urban drainage sizing
  • Studies for environmental permits
  • Construction-phase water management

Why it matters

  • An undersized culvert means a collapsed road
  • A bridge without a hydraulic study is a civil liability
  • A developer without a flood study loses the permit
  • POMCA and EIA require verifiable models
  • Climate adaptation is already a regulatory requirement
  • Designing by eye costs more when it rains
02 · HYDROLOGY

How much water arrives?

We characterize your project's watershed and calculate the expected discharges for each return period. IDEAM data, hydrologic modeling and cross-validation.

Watershed analysis GIS

  • Delineation with a high-resolution DEM
  • Morphometric parameters (area, slope, length)
  • Time of concentration (Kirpich, Témez, Giandotti)
  • Land cover, land use, infiltration (CN)
  • Thematic maps in QGIS / ArcGIS

Hydrometeorological analysis

  • Nearby IDEAM stations and historical records
  • IDF curves (Intensity-Duration-Frequency)
  • Frequency analysis (Gumbel, Log-Pearson III)
  • Return periods of 2 / 5 / 10 / 25 / 50 / 100 years
  • Design hyetographs (SCS, alternating block)

Rainfall-runoff modeling HEC-HMS

  • SCS method (CN)
  • Synthetic unit hydrograph
  • Flood routing
  • Calibration with observed events (when data exist)
  • Sensitivity analysis

Design discharges

  • Rational method for small watersheds
  • HEC-HMS modeling for medium and large watersheds
  • Cross-validation between methods
  • Table of Q vs Tr with uncertainty analysis
03 · HYDRAULICS

How does the water move?

Once we have the discharges, we model the hydraulic behavior: water levels, velocities, shear stress, submergence and scour.

  1. 1D modeling of rivers and channels One-dimensional HEC-RAS for flood routing, gradually varied flow profiles and flood extents along the channel.
  2. 2D floodplain modeling HEC-RAS 2D for flat terrain, deltas and deltaic plains, developments near rivers. Accurate depth, velocity and hazard maps.
  3. Hydraulic design of crossing structures Box culverts, pipe culverts, bridges. Capacity check, submergence, allowable velocities, backwater control.
  4. Scour analysis General and local scour at piers and abutments. Lischtvan-Lebediev and Maza-Echavarría methods, HEC-18 (Pier and Abutment Scour).
  5. Design of control structures Energy dissipators, gabions, groynes, levees, detention ponds, overflow spillways.
  6. Flood studies Flood extents by return period for POMCA, EIA, emergency plans and land-use restrictions.

06 · PROCESS

From data to flood extents.

The timeline depends on the watershed and the level of detail, but the process is always the same. No shortcuts.

  1. Data collection · 1 to 2 weeks IDEAM data, DEM, official cartography, nearby stations, historical rainfall and discharge records, existing bathymetry.
  2. Reconnaissance visit · 1 day Inspection of the channel, control sections, high-water marks, existing structures and critical points. Complementary topographic survey if applicable.
  3. Hydrologic analysis · 1 week Watershed characterization, IDF analysis, HEC-HMS modeling, discharge validation with the rational method where applicable.
  4. Hydraulic modeling · 2 to 3 weeks Building a 1D or 2D model as needed, calibration, simulation for all return periods, flood maps, scour analysis.
  5. Design and report · 1 week Sizing of hydraulic structures, drawings, technical report, appendices. Internal peer review.
  6. Technical defense We defend the study before the regional environmental authority (CAR), independent oversight and the contracting agency. Support for permits and filings.
