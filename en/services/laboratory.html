01 · SCOPE

Without a laboratory there is no reliable engineering.

The laboratory is where engineering becomes verifiable. Every sample comes in under chain of custody, every piece of equipment is calibrated, every result is signed by the responsible engineer. Without this, design is guesswork and on-site quality control is worthless paper.

What we deliver

  • Physical tests: moisture content, grain size, Atterberg limits
  • Mechanical tests: Proctor, CBR, compression, shear
  • On-site quality control: field density, compaction
  • Traceable chain of custody
  • Results signed by the responsible engineer
  • Raw data for audit

When it is critical

  • Soil studies for building permits
  • Road pavement design
  • On-site compaction control
  • Quality of quarry aggregates
  • Technical audit of existing works
  • Technical defense before independent oversight
02 · PHYSICAL TESTS

Material characterization.

We identify what the soil is: grain size, plasticity, moisture content, relative density. The basis for classifying it and predicting its behavior.

Basic characterization

  • Natural moisture content (INV E-122)
  • Sieve analysis (D-422)
  • Hydrometer analysis (D-422 / E-123)
  • Specific gravity (D-854)
  • Organic content

Plasticity and classification

  • Liquid limit (D-4318)
  • Plastic limit (D-4318)
  • Plasticity index
  • USCS / AASHTO classification
  • Identification of expansive soils
03 · MECHANICAL TESTS

Strength, compaction and deformation.

  1. Standard and modified Proctor (D-698 / D-1557)Determination of maximum dry density and optimum moisture content. Basis for on-site compaction control.
  2. Soaked CBR (D-1883)Strength for pavement design. 1, 4, 7 days per protocol. A critical input for road design.
  3. Unconfined compression (D-2166)Quick strength in clays. Useful for preliminary analysis and consulting.
  4. Direct shear CD/CU/UU (D-3080)Strength parameters c and φ. For slope and retaining wall stability analysis.
  5. Triaxial CU (D-4767)Saturated cohesive soils with pore pressure measurement. Advanced seismic analysis.
  6. One-dimensional consolidation (D-2435)Settlement from primary and secondary consolidation. Critical in soft clays.
04 · ON-SITE QUALITY CONTROL

The laboratory that travels to the site.

When construction is under way, the laboratory moves to the site: day-to-day compaction control, material verification and early detection of deviations.

Field density

  • Sand cone (INV E-161)
  • Nuclear density gauge
  • Check against the reference Proctor
  • Daily report to the resident engineer and project management

Other controls

  • Quick on-site grain size checks
  • Field moisture content
  • Dynamic cone penetrometer (DCP)
  • Visual inspection with engineering judgment
  • Immediate recommendations if a test fails

06 · PROCESS

From sample to signed result.

  1. Receipt and custodyEach sample is labeled with boring, depth, date and operator. Photo log. Internal code assigned.
  2. ConditioningControlled storage, preparation per the test protocol, drying or saturation as required.
  3. Test executionCalibrated equipment, certified technician, continuous recording. Retesting if results are inconsistent.
  4. Analysis and reportingCalculations per standard, plots, comparison with reference values, technical conclusions.
  5. Sign-off by the responsible engineerThe responsible engineer reviews, signs with a professional license number and takes technical responsibility.
