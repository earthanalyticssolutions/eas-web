01 · SCOPE

Investigate before repairing.

A geotechnical or structural failure is not an event, it is a symptom of a process that started months or years earlier. Repairing without understanding the cause is covering up the problem. EAS carries out forensic investigation to deliver a defensible technical diagnosis and a corrective plan that targets the root cause.

What we deliver

  • Detailed inspection with georeferenced damage
  • Technical hypothesis of the root cause
  • Verification through exploration or testing where applicable
  • Quantification of current damage
  • Prognosis of how it will evolve
  • Prioritized corrective plan
  • Signed technical report to support expert witness work

When we get called

  • Cracks in new or existing buildings
  • Subsidence in floors or pavements
  • A moving slope or a displaced wall
  • A failing retaining wall
  • A road with recurring potholes
  • Unexplained seepage
  • Technical dispute with the builder or neighbors
02 · TYPES OF DISTRESS

Every failure has its signature.

  1. Cracks in walls and slabsDifferential settlement, soil swelling, concrete shrinkage, overloading, thermal failure. The shape, direction and evolution of the crack tell the story.
  2. Subsidence and uneven levelsOverall or differential settlement, soil collapse, fill failure, underground cavities. Periodic surveys reveal the dynamics.
  3. Slope movementsRotational or translational slides, flows, falls. Identification of the failure surface, triggering factors and rate of movement.
  4. Retaining wall failureOverturning, sliding, bearing capacity failure, drainage failure. Quantification of the actual earth pressure versus the design value.
  5. Failing pavementsRutting, cracking, raveling, recurring potholes. Identification of the failing layer (subgrade, base, wearing course).
  6. Leaks and seepageSource of the water, flow path, mechanism. Implications for the stability and durability of the structure.
03 · FORENSIC METHODOLOGY

Investigating like a technical detective.

  1. Interviews and backgroundProject history, construction dates, materials, relevant events (rainfall, earthquakes, nearby works), evolution of the damage.
  2. Detailed visual inspectionDamage mapping and quantification, georeferenced photo log, crack width, direction and depth. Evidence catalog.
  3. MonitoringPlaster tell-tales, crack gauges, inclinometers, survey leveling. If the damage is active, we measure it over time.
  4. Complementary explorationTest pits, borings and tests where the diagnosis requires them. Geophysics to detect cavities. Sampling of suspect materials.
  5. Analysis and hypothesisCross-checking evidence, ruling out inconsistent hypotheses, validating the most likely one. Quantifying the failure mechanism.
  6. PrognosisWithout intervention: what will happen? Over what time frame? With what consequences? Quantification of residual risk.
04 · COMMON CAUSES

What is usually behind it.

Geotechnical causes

  • Deficient or missing soil study
  • Undersized foundation
  • Unidentified expansive soils
  • Poorly compacted fills
  • Changes in the water table
  • Nearby excavations causing damage

Structural causes

  • Design not compliant with NSR-10 (Colombian Building Code)
  • Change of building use
  • Sustained overloading
  • Low-quality materials
  • Poorly executed construction details

Environmental / water causes

  • Deficient surface drainage
  • Leaks from pipes
  • Perimeter erosion
  • Saturation of the foundation soil
  • Wet-dry cycles in clays

External / human causes

  • Vibrations from nearby construction
  • Loads not considered in the design
  • Unauthorized modifications
  • Lack of preventive maintenance
  • Undetected previous seismic events
05 · CORRECTIVE PLAN

Repair the cause, not the symptom.

Once the root cause is understood, we design the intervention. The corrective works must address the cause before or at the same time as the symptom; otherwise the damage comes back.

Typical solutions

  • Foundation underpinning (micropiles, jet grouting)
  • Deep perimeter drainage
  • Slope stabilization (anchors, drains, berms)
  • Rebuilding walls with correct dimensions
  • Crack injection and structural strengthening
  • Replacement of failing pavement layers

What we recommend first

  • Stop the triggering factor (drainage, water, overloading)
  • Monitor the evolution during the intervention
  • Staged intervention with verification
  • Follow-up maintenance with a clear plan
  • If the area is not viable, resettlement
06 · PROCESS

From the first call to the plan.

  1. Initial visit · 24-48hReconnaissance of the situation, preliminary survey, background collection. Quote with schedule.
  2. Detailed inspection · 1 weekComplete mapping, monitoring if applicable, photo log, georeferencing.
  3. Complementary exploration · 1-2 weeksTest pits, tests and geophysics where the diagnosis requires them. Collection of additional evidence.
  4. Analysis and hypothesis · 1-2 weeksCross-checking information, defining the root cause, quantifying the failure mechanism, producing a prognosis.
  5. Corrective plan · 1 weekIntervention design, alternatives, preliminary budget, schedule for the corrective works.
  6. Signed technical reportComplete technical document, defensible before independent oversight, in court as expert evidence or in a technical dispute.
