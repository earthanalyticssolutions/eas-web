01 · SCOPE

The pavement that lasts is the one that was designed.

An undersized pavement looks the same on day one. The difference shows after 2 years: rutting, cracking, potholes, constant repairs. EAS designs pavements with a verifiable methodology (INVIAS, AASHTO, AUSTROADS) and backs every decision with data.

What we deliver

  • Geotechnical characterization of the subgrade
  • Traffic study (AADT, ESALs)
  • Complete structural design
  • Material specifications
  • Detail and joint drawings
  • Surface drainage and subdrainage
  • Unit price analysis (APU) and bill of quantities

What we DON'T do

  • "Standard" designs without site characterization
  • Assuming CBR without measuring it
  • Copying designs from other roads without adjustment
  • Ignoring the actual expected traffic
  • Forgetting drainage (the #1 cause of failure)
02 · CHARACTERIZATION

Soil, traffic and climate: the 3 design inputs.

  1. SubgradeTest pits, CBR, grain size, Atterberg limits, Proctor. Identification of expansive, collapsible or problematic soils. Characterization by homogeneous segments.
  2. TrafficVehicle counts, classification, 10-20 year projection. Equivalent single axle loads (ESALs). Distinction between commercial and light traffic.
  3. ClimatePrecipitation, moisture cycles, temperature. Regional coefficients for AASHTO design. Frost or drought zones affect the materials.
  4. Available materialsIdentification of local quarries and aggregate sources, quality tests. Design optimized for what is actually available.
03 · PAVEMENT TYPES

Each with its own logic.

Flexible pavement HMA

  • Asphalt surface course + base + subbase
  • AASHTO 93 / Shell / INVIAS empirical method
  • For medium and high traffic
  • More tolerant of settlement

Rigid pavement PCC

  • Portland cement concrete slab
  • AASHTO 93 / PCA method
  • For high traffic or industrial use
  • Greater durability, less maintenance

Placa huella (concrete wheel tracks)

  • Concrete pavement for rural areas
  • Low cost · high durability
  • Design per the INVIAS Tertiary Roads Manual
  • Thickness + gutter + loaded structure

Interlocking pavement

  • Concrete pavers on a sand bed
  • Plazas, parking lots, pedestrian streets
  • Aesthetics + functionality
  • Design per NTC + ICONTEC (Colombian technical standards)
04 · ASSESSMENT AND REHABILITATION

Existing roads that need intervention.

Distress inventory, identification of causes, design of the right rehabilitation. Repaving is not always the answer: sometimes it is drainage, sometimes stabilizing the subgrade, sometimes only replacing the wearing course.

Condition assessment

  • Detailed distress inventory (PCI or equivalent)
  • Root cause identification
  • Structural evaluation with FWD / Benkelman beam
  • Core recovery for analysis
  • Characterization of the existing subgrade

Rehabilitation strategies

  • Overlay with or without milling
  • In-place recycling
  • Full reconstruction
  • Stabilization with lime, cement or asphalt
  • Drainage improvement (sometimes that is all it takes)
05 · PROCESS

From quote to on-site quality control.

  1. Initial assessment · 48hSite visit, drawing review, scope definition. Quote with a realistic schedule.
  2. Field and laboratory · 2-3 weeksTest pits, CBR, vehicle counts, tests. For existing roads: distress inventory and structural evaluation.
  3. Analysis and design · 1-2 weeksHomogeneous segmentation, structural design, drainage, specifications, drawings.
  4. Signed technical reportTechnical document with methodology, justification of assumptions, drawings, APU and construction recommendations.
  5. Optional on-site supportOn-site quality control, field density tests, technical advice to the resident engineer, adjustments during construction.
