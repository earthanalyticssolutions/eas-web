01 · SCOPE

The water you cannot see also shapes the project.

A high water table can flood excavations, compromise foundations, invalidate road designs or alter slope stability. And an aquifer can be a strategic resource or an environmental liability. Hydrogeology answers these questions with verifiable data.

What we deliver

  • Aquifer characterization (type, thickness, conductivity)
  • Piezometric levels and flow direction
  • Pumping tests and hydrogeological parameters
  • Physicochemical quality of groundwater
  • Design of production or dewatering wells
  • Flow and transport modeling in MODFLOW
  • Studies for groundwater concessions

When it is critical

  • Deep excavations with a high water table
  • Basements over shallow aquifers
  • Construction near water bodies
  • Need for water supply from a well
  • Suspected soil or water contamination
  • Concession permit from ANA / environmental authority
  • Environmental impact studies with a water component
02 · HYDROGEOLOGICAL CHARACTERIZATION

We map the water in the subsurface.

We combine regional geological information with test drilling, in-situ tests and piezometric monitoring to define the hydrogeological model of the site.

Inventory and exploration FIELD

  • Inventory of nearby wells, springs and dug wells
  • Test drilling with sampling
  • Continuous lithological logging
  • Identification of hydrogeological units
  • Aquifer depth and thickness

Piezometric monitoring

  • Piezometers with automatic dataloggers
  • Water table time series
  • Seasonal variability analysis
  • Groundwater flow directions
  • Hydraulic gradients

Field tests FIELD

  • Pumping tests (Theis, Cooper-Jacob, Neuman)
  • Slug tests for hydraulic conductivity
  • Injection or recovery tests
  • Flow gauging at existing intakes
  • Tracer tests where applicable

Complementary geophysics

  • Vertical electrical soundings (VES)
  • 2D / 3D electrical resistivity tomography
  • Proton magnetic resonance
  • Detection of contaminant plumes
  • Mapping of saturated zones
03 · WELL DESIGN

Production, dewatering or monitoring wells.

Each type of well has its own design logic. We handle the sizing, the technical specifications and the follow-up during drilling and well development.

  1. Deep wells for water supply Hydraulic design, screen and gravel pack selection, casing, yield test, pumping equipment, sanitary seal, maintenance plan.
  2. Dewatering wells (well-points and deep wells) For excavations below the water table: required pumping rate, system layout, cone of depression modeling, real-time monitoring.
  3. Monitoring wells and piezometers To track the water table, water quality and contaminant plumes. Specifications per ASTM standards and environmental guidelines.
  4. Dug wells and hand-dug wells Technical design for rural areas or countryside developments for domestic use. Compliance with minimum depth, sanitary seal and setback from contamination sources.
  5. Injection or managed recharge wells For sustainable aquifer management: artificial recharge, saltwater intrusion control, closing the treated-water loop.
04 · GROUNDWATER QUALITY

Having water is not enough. It has to be usable.

Physicochemical and bacteriological analysis to determine whether the water is fit for human consumption, irrigation, industrial or environmental use. Traceable sampling and an accredited laboratory.

Physicochemical parameters

  • Electrical conductivity, pH, temperature
  • Total dissolved solids (TDS)
  • Major ions: Ca, Mg, Na, K, HCO₃, SO₄, Cl
  • Nitrates, nitrites, ammonium
  • Iron, manganese, hardness
  • Piper / Stiff / Schoeller diagrams

Microbiological

  • Total and fecal coliforms
  • E. coli
  • Heterotrophic bacteria
  • Pseudomonas (where applicable)

Specific contaminants

  • Heavy metals (As, Pb, Cd, Cr, Hg)
  • Total hydrocarbons and BTEX
  • Organochlorine / organophosphate pesticides
  • Isotopic tracers where applicable

Regulatory application

  • Resolution 2115/2007 (drinking water · Colombia)
  • Decree 1594/1984 (water use)
  • WHO / EPA for international benchmarks
  • Comparison with reference values
  • Conclusions on water fitness for use

06 · PROCESS

From inventory to model.

  1. Regional data collection · 2 weeks Information from IDEAM, ANLA, IGAC and the local environmental authority. Inventory of surrounding wells, regional hydrogeological data.
  2. Field work · 2 to 4 weeks Test drilling, piezometer installation, monitoring, pumping tests, geophysics. All under chain of custody.
  3. Laboratory · 2 to 3 weeks Physicochemical and microbiological analyses in an IDEAM-accredited laboratory. Certified results backed by chain of custody.
  4. Analysis and interpretation · 2 weeks Building the hydrogeological conceptual model, parameter calculation, water quality interpretation, fitness-for-use assessment, recommendations.
  5. Numerical modeling · 2 to 4 weeks (if applicable) MODFLOW model construction, calibration with field data, scenario simulation (abstraction, dewatering, contaminant transport).
  6. Report, drawings and support Delivery of the signed technical report, drawings, appendices and open model files. Technical defense before the environmental authority when required.

Construction

  • Basements over shallow aquifers
  • Foundations below the water table
  • Deep drainage design
  • Stabilization of saturated slopes

Water supply

  • Industrial water intake
  • Supply for countryside developments
  • Groundwater concession
  • Renewal of existing concessions

Environmental

  • Environmental impact assessments (EIA)
  • Environmental management plan (PMA)
  • Hydrogeological model for permitting

  • Characterization of contaminated soils

Special cases

  • Forensic contamination investigation
  • Studies for mining and oil and gas
  • Managed aquifer recharge
  • Climate change adaptation
